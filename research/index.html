Research & Activism

Visibility, Archive,
and the Politics of Memory

In my research I encountered audiovisual works that once circulated but are now very difficult to find, along with others that were directly censored or removed from any possibility of reaching an audience. The distance between production, reception and memory is not accidental: it is the result of economic, institutional and political decisions. My research practice seeks to intervene in that distance, recovering, circulating and debating the work of artists and ideas that allow us to think the present from places the mainstream does not enable.

01 · Circulation

Luz Mala

Film debate & circulation · Buenos Aires · 2025 –

An independent film screening and debate initiative founded in 2025, organised by graduates of Image and Sound Design at UBA. Luz Mala starts from a premise: the absence of a film from distribution circuits is never neutral. Through thematic series (Palestinian cinema, feminist cinema, Latin American cinema), it builds the conditions for collective debate around films that dominant platforms do not distribute and the industry does not recover.

Silbando Bembas

Militant cinema collective · Argentina · 2026 –

A militant cinema collective based in Buenos Aires, Rosario and Mar del Plata, active since 2007. It does not produce films for festivals or the market; instead, it creates political tools in service of concrete struggles, whether for workers on strike, communities in conflict or organised social movements. Each project responds to a specific political objective and audience. Authorship is collective at every stage: pre-production, shooting, editing, distribution and theoretical reflection on practice. Since 2026, I have participated in scriptwriting, editing and distribution.

02 · Research

IDIS

Investigaciones sobre el Diseño de Imagen y Sonido · Research archive · 2021 –

My research work at IDIS was driven by the intention to expand the archive by incorporating countries and artistic contexts from the Americas not yet represented. While researching artistic production in South and Central America, I identified recurring themes related to censorship, forced displacement, restricted mobility, and the proximity of violence and death. These conditions shape not only the content of the works but also their modes of production, circulation, and survival. Working within such environments reveals how artistic and design practices emerge under extreme material and political constraints, where visibility is fragile and preservation is scarce.
